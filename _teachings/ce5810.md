### Objectives

1. Introduce urban transportation planning as a systems-based process that links land use, travel behaviour and transport supply, and place it within India's urban transport policy framework.
2. Teach students to design travel surveys and to prepare the zoning systems, network representations and base-year data that travel demand models need.
3. Equip students to formulate, calibrate and validate each stage of the sequential (four-step) travel demand model: trip generation, trip distribution, modal split and traffic assignment.
4. Enable students to use these models to forecast future travel demand and to compare alternative transportation plans and policies.
5. Show the limits of the four-step approach and introduce newer approaches such as activity-based and land use–transport interaction models.

### Course Content

- Urban transportation planning concepts – urban travel characteristics and problems; systems approach to the planning process; goals, objectives and evaluation criteria; overview of the sequential travel demand modelling framework; Indian context – National Urban Transport Policy and Comprehensive Mobility Plans
- Travel demand data – study area delineation and traffic analysis zones; network coding; household travel and origin–destination surveys; cordon and screen-line counts; sampling, expansion and validation of survey data
- Trip generation modelling – trip purposes and classification; variables influencing trip production and attraction; zonal and household-level regression analysis; category (cross-classification) analysis; balancing of productions and attractions
- Trip distribution modelling – factors governing trip distribution; growth-factor methods (uniform, average, Fratar and Furness); gravity models – singly and doubly constrained forms and deterrence functions; calibration of gravity models; entropy-maximisation derivation
- Modal split modelling – factors influencing mode choice; trip-end and trip-interchange models; random utility theory; binary and multinomial logit models; maximum likelihood estimation; the IIA property and nested logit; elasticities and value of travel time
- Traffic assignment – link performance functions; all-or-nothing, incremental and capacity-restraint assignment; Wardrop's principles – user equilibrium and system optimum; Beckmann's formulation and the Frank–Wolfe algorithm; introduction to stochastic and transit assignment
- Model application and evaluation – model validation and feedback between stages; base-year calibration and future-year forecasting; scenario analysis and evaluation of alternative plans; introduction to activity-based models, land use–transport interaction and urban freight demand
