  1. PostDoc
    Optimizing Crowdsourced Delivery Systems for Perishable Goods
    Anmol Pahwa
    Jul 2027
    Abs

    Perishable goods delivery presents unique logistical challenges where product quality deteriorates rapidly during transport, requiring precise timing and temperature control to maintain consumer satisfaction. These complexities have been amplified by quick-commerce platforms delivering groceries and meals within 15–30 minute windows, creating demands for algorithmic solutions that handle workforce volatility and product degradation constraints. Platforms face inefficiencies due to unpredictable agent availability and inadequate routing optimization that fails to account for time-sensitive quality requirements. This research develops an integrated framework combining machine learning (ML) and microeconomic modeling to optimize crowdsourced delivery systems for perishable goods, addressing gaps where traditional logistics models prove inadequate in dynamic marketplace environments. The approach represents a shift from isolated optimization domains toward comprehensive algorithmic integration that addresses supply uncertainty and demand variability in crowdsourced networks. The supply prediction module employs ensemble ML algorithms to forecast delivery agent participation patterns by analyzing behavioral economics factors, including incentive structures, opportunity costs, and external market conditions. The demand forecasting component utilizes recurrent neural networks with attention mechanisms to capture temporal dependencies in consumer purchasing patterns, incorporating lead time preferences, price sensitivity, and quality degradation expectations for perishable goods categories. The framework operates through integrated data pipelines where real-time demand forecasting triggers supply prediction algorithms, which feed into the routing optimization engine. When orders are placed, demand updates inform supply availability assessments within delivery time windows, converging into route assignments that minimize spoilage while maximizing stakeholder satisfaction. The core innovation integrates economic constraints into vehicle routing optimization algorithms, ensuring route planning optimizes both operational efficiency and market-driven factors, including dynamic pricing and agent compensation. The routing engine considers product shelf life, temperature requirements, and delivery urgency to minimize spoilage while maximizing satisfaction across all stakeholders. This interdisciplinary approach bridges economic theory, machine learning, and operations research to advance knowledge in crowdsourced delivery optimization while demonstrating measurable improvements in delivery efficiency, cost effectiveness, and stakeholder satisfaction for perishable goods logistics.

  1. MS
    Modelling the Spatial Structure of Service Catchment Areas in Q-Commerce Last-Mile Delivery Systems
    Ranjith Kanna , Anmol Pahwa, and Bhargava Chilukuri
    Jul 2026
    Abs

    Rapid growth of Q-commerce has produced a dense network of small last-mile delivery facilities (“dark stores”) operating under strict time and distance constraints. Unlike conventional logistics systems, Q-commerce facilities serve compact, highly time-sensitive urban catchments, where even small changes in facility location can significantly alter service coverage, overlap, and operational efficiency. However, existing location-planning approaches largely assume highly regular service regions, typically circular catchments, which do not adequately capture how the shape, structure, and fragmentation of service catchments emerge in dense urban environments. This study aims to develop a spatial modelling framework to analyse how service catchment geometry impacts the configuration (number and location) of Q-commerce dark stores. Instead of committing to one idealised geometry, the framework will test a small set of shape families that span the main mechanisms by which real catchments emerge: (i) distance-limited regions (as a baseline), (ii) tessellating, repeatable partitions that approximate dense facility layouts (grid- or honeycomb-like), (iii) proximity-based partitions (Voronoi-type) that arise naturally when customers choose the nearest facility and when facilities are repositioned toward the centre of their served region, and (iv) non-periodic partitions (aperiodic tiling) with “irregular but structured” layouts. These families cover both Euclidean and Rectilinear travel environments and allow the model to represent elongation, fragmentation, and boundary effects without over-specifying a single polygon template. The framework will incorporate constrained or truncated regions with administrative boundaries, barriers, or corridor-like service structures, while remaining consistent with key urban planning constraints. Building on these ideas, the study will quantify how catchment geometry influences overlap, coverage gaps, effective travel distances, and the robustness of promised delivery times. An optimisation layer will generate candidate facility configurations, while a multi-criteria decision module will prioritise configurations that balance operational efficiency and service reliability against city-planning constraints (land-use compatibility, residential exposure, and neighbourhood accessibility). The expected outcome is a transferable decision-support framework linking catchment geometry, location optimisation, and urban planning considerations for regulating Q-commerce last-mile systems in dense cities.

  2. BTech
    Adaptive Bus Fleet Management – Enhancing Efficiency and Resilience under Disruptions
    Aurovind Sadangi , and Anmol Pahwa
    Jun 2026
    Abs

    Urban bus systems play a critical role in supporting daily mobility for large populations in major cities, (e.g., Chennai (India) and Rotterdam (The Netherlands). However, this system faces several challenges leading to disturbances and disruptions. This can range from special events to natural calamities, leading to reduced capacity to road closures or other system constraints. These lead to travel time uncertainty, depot inaccessibility, and even road closures. Despite the growing availability of real-time data sources such as traffic feeds, vehicle GPS tracking, and weather information, bus operations during such events, varying in both intensity and duration, largely rely on static schedules and reactive decision-making. This project proposes the development of an adaptive digital twin framework for bus operations during disaster events, integrating real-time travel time data, traffic information, and weather inputs to support dynamic operational decision-making under disruption conditions. The digital twin will incorporate predictive travel-time estimation and a rolling-horizon optimization algorithm capable of dynamically adjusting vehicle allocation, dispatch intervals, and routing decisions while respecting operational constraints. By comparing adaptive fleet management strategies with conventional ad hoc approaches, the project will quantify the benefits of the proposed adaptive management in terms of service reliability, operational efficiency, and passenger accessibility .

  4. BTech
    Graph Neural Network-Based Tour Length Estimation for the Travelling Salesman Problem
    Abshishek Rai , and Anmol Pahwa
    Jun 2026
    Abs

    Tour length estimation plays an important role in logistics planning, where fast approximations are needed across large numbers of scenarios without running a full optimization each time. Classical Continuous Approximation (CA) methods, such as the Beardwood-Halton-Hammersley (BHH) formula, provide analytical estimates but assume customers are uniformly distributed, an assumption that often breaks down in real delivery networks where demand clusters spatially around dense areas. This project explores whether Graph Neural Networks can learn to predict TSP and VRP tour lengths more accurately than classical formulas, particularly on non-uniform and clustered customer distributions. By representing each problem instance as a graph, where nodes are customer locations and edges capture spatial relationships, a GNN can potentially learn structural patterns that fixed analytical formulas cannot express. The model will be trained on synthetically generated instances using optimal tour lengths from an exact solver as ground truth. Prediction accuracy will be evaluated against the BHH formula and a standard feedforward neural network baseline, across both uniform and clustered distributions, to assess where and by how much the graph-based approach improves on existing methods.

  5. BTech
    Task Acceptance, Wage Elasticity, and Platform Mobility: A Multi-Margin Analysis of Ride-Hailing and Parcel-Delivery Gig Workers in India
    Akshith Nemalipuri , and Anmol Pahwa
    Jun 2026
    Abs

    Gig work has become a major source of urban livelihoods in India, particularly in ride-hailing and parcel-delivery services. In such arrangements, platforms act as marketplaces matching supply with demand through price signals – service cost for users and payouts with incentives for workers. Contingent on these remunerations, workers decide for themselves which tasks to accept, how long to work, and what platforms to log in to. These decisions jointly determine service reliability for platforms and daily earnings for workers. Yet labor supply in gig work is poorly understood: platforms design payouts algorithmically, albeit with limited capability to anticipate the full range of worker responses (task acceptance, wage elasticity, and platform mobility); workers bear the entire underlying costs (vehicle financing and operating expenses) that payouts conceal; and most importantly, policymakers set welfare rules with sparse evidence on market dynamics. Without reliable estimates of these responses, the effects of payouts/incentives and welfare standards on worker incomes and service availability cannot be predicted. To this end, this project will develop and estimate an integrated framework of three behavioral models linking decision margins at different time scales – Model 1: Task Acceptance; Model 2: Short-Run Wage Elasticity; Model 3: Long-Run Platform Mobility. To develop these models, this work will carry out a joint RP-SP panel survey of about 1,000 workers from partner platforms. The findings from this work will help platforms design incentive structures, such as compensation for pickup distance, idle time, and trips to low-demand destinations, that improve task acceptance. Further, wage-elasticity estimates will enable platforms to tailor pay to different workers to sustain short-run availability, while platform-mobility estimates will reveal the earnings needed for long-run retention. Importantly, the findings will also give policymakers evidence on how gig workers respond to pay changes, informing welfare standards and schemes under national and state gig-worker legislation.

  6. BTech
    Exploring Statistical Models for Continuous Approximation of Capacitated Vehicle Routing Problem (CVRP) Tour Lengths using Symbolic Regression
    Saharsh Yadav , and Anmol Pahwa
    Jun 2026
    Abs

    Strategic last-mile distribution planning needs quick estimates of the total delivery tour length from representative, rather than exact, information about customers. Running a Vehicle Routing Problem (VRP) solver for every candidate network configuration is computationally impractical, while classical Continuous Approximation (CA) formulas assume uniformly scattered customers with identical demands and lose accuracy in realistic distribution environments. This project aims to develop an interpretable, closed-form CA model for the tour length of the Capacitated Vehicle Routing Problem (CVRP) across diverse spatial and demand settings. During this semester, a library of synthetic environments was built by introducing two new families of customer distributions, multi-cluster and Beta (U-shaped), for both spatial dispersion and demand, alongside the uniform, triangular, normal and exponential families. Combining six spatial and six demand patterns with sampled service-region sizes, depot locations, vehicle capacities and customer counts (100–500) yielded 1,080 CVRP instances. Each instance was solved with an Adaptive Large Neighborhood Search (ALNS) metaheuristic to obtain near-optimal tour lengths. Validation on 68 CVRPLIB benchmark instances (680 runs across ten random seeds) showed median gaps to the best-known solutions of 1.05% (best of ten seeds), 1.57% (mean) and 2.07% (worst seed). The next phase will apply symbolic regression to this dataset, using moments of customer nearest-neighbor distances and demands together with depot distance, to discover closed-form tour length equations, which will then be tested on independent benchmark instances against the classical Daganzo (1984) model.

  7. BTech
    Analysing Active Goods Mobility (AGM) in Chennai: Opportunities and Challenges for Sustainable Urban Freight
    Shivathmikasri R H , and Anmol Pahwa
    Jun 2026
    Abs

    The increasing adoption of battery-electric buses comes with challenges regarding reliable energy management and route-level operation planning, which is largely due to the fact that energy consumption of an electric bus largely depends on its operating environment and conditions. Accurate SOC estimation is thus required for determining route feasibility, energy consumption and operational planning. Existing literature is divided into data-driven and physics-based approaches, whereas data-driven approaches are generally data-intensive, requiring large amounts of operational data, while only little work has been done on physics-based SOC estimation under data-scarce conditions, particularly for India. In this project, we propose to develop a non-causal white box approach for physics-based SOC estimation specific to DTC bus routes in Delhi. Our proposed methodology will reconstruct routes using mapping and routing APIs and will gather geographical and traffic-related parameters in the absence of operational data from DTC buses. We will create speed-acceleration profiles of a route based on the geometry of the route, road gradient, stops, traffic and driving behavior. We may use traffic simulation tools like SUMO in certain cases. We will integrate these operating conditions into a physics-based energy consumption model taking into account all parameters including traction force, aerodynamic drag, rolling resistance, grade, regenerative brake and auxiliary energy consumption such as HVAC system. Energy consumption will be used to determine route-level SOC consumption and also determine the influence of critical parameters such as speed, acceleration, stops, road grade and traffic conditions. The objective of this work will be to develop a data-efficient SOC estimation framework specific to Indian urban environments.

  9. MTech
    Freight Trip Generation Modelling for E-commerce Deliveries in Bangalore
    Millenium Mahaseth , Jash Shah , and Anmol Pahwa
    Jun 2026
    Abs

    The rapid growth of e-commerce has changed the way goods are bought, sold and delivered in cities. With more people purchasing products online, the number of deliveries made within urban areas has also increased. This has led to more freight vehicle movements, particularly for last-mile deliveries. E-commerce establishments do not all operate in the same way. Their freight activity can vary depending on the type of commerce, number of packages handled, delivery personnel, vehicles used, delivery distance and other operational characteristics. Understanding these differences is important for estimating freight demand and planning urban transportation systems. This study looks at the freight demand generated by e-commerce-related establishments in Bengaluru. The analysis is based on establishment-level survey data collected from 156 facilities located across different zones of Bengaluru. The establishments covered in the dataset include conventional commerce, quick commerce and bulk commerce. The data provide information on several aspects of their operations, including commerce type, sales channel, distribution structure, facility area, packages handled, delivery personnel, vehicle availability, vehicle type, ICE and EV usage, weekly vehicle trips, delivery distances, parking facilities and EV charging infrastructure. The first stage of the study is to understand the characteristics of these establishments and their freight activities using descriptive analysis. This helps in identifying how freight activity varies between different types of establishments. The study then examines the relationship between freight trips and different establishment and operational characteristics. Weekly freight vehicle trips are considered as a measure of freight trip generation, and variables such as facility area, packages handled, delivery personnel, commerce type, sales channel and vehicle-related characteristics are examined as possible factors influencing freight demand. Based on the observed relationships in the data, suitable statistical models will be developed to estimate freight trip generation. Different modelling approaches will be compared to determine which approach provides a better representation of the freight activity observed at e-commerce-related establishments in Bengaluru. The findings from the study are expected to provide a better understanding of how e-commerce establishments generate freight trips and how their operational characteristics influence freight movement. The results can also be useful for urban freight planning, delivery and parking management, freight facility planning and planning for electric freight vehicles and charging infrastructure in Bengaluru.

  10. PhD
    Assessing Wider Economic Benefits of Transportation Infrastructure Investments (WEB of TransIT)
    Hana Aboobacker , and Anmol Pahwa
    Jul 2026
    Abs

    India’s growing economy and consumption demand are placing increasing pressure on its freight system, which remains heavily dependent on long-haul trucking. While road freight is central to economic growth, it also increases crude oil dependence, greenhouse gas emissions, and local air pollution. Although zero-emission freight technologies offer long-term mitigation potential, their adoption is constrained by technological, infrastructural, and economic barriers. In this context, India’s emerging Dedicated Freight Corridor (DFC) network offers a strategic opportunity to shift freight from road to rail, reduce logistics costs, improve supply-chain reliability, and support sustainable growth. Beyond these direct benefits, freight rail infrastructure can generate wider economic benefits through productivity gains, improved interregional trade linkages, changes in commodity prices, factor returns, household income, tax revenues, public expenditure, and investment decisions. This study aims to estimate the Wider Economic Benefits (WEB) of Transportation Infrastructure (TRANSIT) in India by assessing how the proposed DFC network affects regional household income, factor returns, and fiscal outcomes through changes in logistics costs. It will further develop an optimal DFC network design that maximizes national welfare under infrastructure and fiscal constraints, and compare its aggregate and distributional impacts with the proposed network. Methodologically, the study will develop a nationwide Computable General Equilibrium model incorporating sectoral input-output relationships, commodity consumption behavior, and domestic trade margins to inform strategic freight infrastructure planning in India.

  11. PhD
    Arriving within Time and Energy Budget: A Dynamic Routing Framework for Electric Vehicles in Stochastic Networks
    Kaviyapriya K N , and Anmol Pahwa
    Jul 2026
    Abs

    The transportation sector is one of the most resource intensive industry, contributing 30% to global energy consumption (REN Secretariat, 2024) thereby reinforcing continued fossil fuel dependence, and 23% to global greenhouse gas emissions (Shukla et al., 2022) thus accelerating climate change. Further, it accounts for a significant share of criteria pollutant emissions – worsening local community health (World Health Organization, 2021). To this end, electric vehicles present a sustainable pathway towards reducing these negative externalities, however, their wide scale adoption is hindered by several practical constraints, with range anxiety posing one of the most significant barriers (Pevec et al., 2019). These challenges are exacerbated by prevailing navigation (route planning) systems. Designed predominantly for internal combustion engine vehicles, conventional routing frameworks optimize for travel time under deterministic assumptions. Consequently, they may propose routes that are energy-infeasible, leading to an electric vehicle getting stranded (Artmeier et al., 2010) – a risk further amplified in stochastic environments where fluctuations in network conditions can significantly undermine route feasibility (Bruni et al., 2020). Therefore, to ensure reliable mobility, this study aims to develop a dynamic (adaptive) routing framework for electric vehicles within stochastic (uncertain) network conditions, incorporating characteristics of the electric powertrain and associated charging infrastructure. To do so, this work proposes Time-Energy Stochastic in-Budget Arrival (TE-SIBA) problem – a finite-horizon Markov Decision Process grounded in the Bellman principle of optimality. To implement this routing framework, the study proposes recursive dynamic programming solution, rendering state-dependent routing policies that maximize the probability of arrival at the destination within prescribed time-energy budgets. Finally, this work will prototype an intelligent navigation system, embedding the TE-SIBA routing framework. With these contributions, this work aims to advance route planning of electric vehicles to help alleviate range anxiety and improve user confidence, thereby supporting broader adoption of electric mobility. Further, this work will form a deployable foundation for next generation of electric vehicle navigation platforms, supporting broader adoption of an economically viable, environmentally efficient, and socially equitable electric mobility. In doing so, the proposed work supports the long-term transition towards sustainable transportation, thus addressing fossil fuel dependence, global climate change, and air-quality impacts of the transportation sector.

    Modelling energy consumption of Unmanned Aerival Vehicles (UAVs) for last-mile delivery
    Preetham Palle , and Anmol Pahwa
    Oct 2025
    Abs

    Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs), commonly known as drones, are emerging as a transformative technology in transportation and logistics. In India, the growth of Drone-as-a-Service (DaaS) platforms across sectors such as logistics, healthcare, and agriculture highlights the increasing operational maturity of UAV systems. Drones offer significant advantages for delivery applications due to their high speeds and ability to bypass ground congestion, thereby reducing delivery times. However, their deployment is constrained by limited payload capacity, high energy requirements at elevated speeds, and strong sensitivity to environmental conditions such as wind and precipitation. This study focuses on the application of UAVs in last-mile delivery, the final stage of the supply chain connecting distribution hubs to end customers. Drone-based delivery systems can operate through multiple configurations, including direct warehouse-to-customer delivery, hybrid truck-drone operations, and larger multi-parcel UAV systems. Existing literature presents mixed conclusions regarding the energy efficiency of drones relative to electric and conventional ground vehicles, largely due to limitations in current modelling approaches. Studies employing detailed UAV energy models often oversimplify logistics operations, while those incorporating realistic delivery systems frequently rely on simplified energy estimations based primarily on travel distance. To address this gap, this study proposes a mesoscopic modelling framework that integrates detailed UAV energy consumption models with realistic representations of last-mile delivery operations. The framework enables a more comprehensive evaluation of drone efficiency, operational feasibility, and sustainability within urban logistics systems.

    Air pollution has emerged as one of the most severe environmental and public health crises facing the world. Children might face development issues which includes impaired lung function and cognitive developments. Pregnant women might face issues in their pregnancy journey which includes low birth weight, premature births, etc. Air pollution also introduces serious impact on the economy, a report from Dalberg Advisors (Berg, n.d.) calculated the total cost of weak environmental regulations and poor emissions practices: USD $95 billion every year, or 40% of the cost of tackling the Covid-19 pandemic in the country - year after year. Per their research, the costs of poor air quality manifest in six ways: lower labor productivity, lower consumer footfall, premature mortality, lower asset productivity, increased health expenses and welfare losses. At the onset of the industrial revolution fossil fuels like coal and petroleum were deemed as the major sources of energy. Humans widely used them to meet the needs of the modern life and developed increasingly robust ways to drill more fossil fuels from the earth’s crust. The combustion of said fuels numerous gases like Carbon Dioxide (CO2), Sulfur Dioxide (SO2), etc., emerge. These emissions contribute to a host of other environmental problems like acid rain, act as greenhouse gases. Thus, the need to measure the quality of air emerged; to communicate the level of pollution in a given area and assess the impact it could have on public health, national economy and global sustenance. Given the rising levels of air pollution levels and policy directive implications, AQI prediction has been an ongoing research topic with several papers authored that utilise statistical and machine learning models to forecast future AQI values for a variety of geographies. This work attempts to use various machine learning models to predict AQI for Indian cities, being trained on various parameters for each city of selected cities i.e., Delhi, Mumbai, Chennai, Kolkata, Bhopal, and Guwahati were chosen to represent all major geographic and climatic zones of India.

  3. BTech
    Simulating Daily Urban Mobility in India: Unravelling the travel patterns
    Arvind Vidhyashankar , and Anmol Pahwa
    Oct 2025
    Abs

    Travel is an induced demand, characterised by individual’s economic and lifestyle choices. As these travel patterns evolve, urban and transportation planning must adapt to meet these changes. Understanding and forecasting travel demand is crucial for effective infrastructure development and policy planning, ensuring that transportation systems align with the needs of the population. Thus, to identify typical travel demand patterns, this study will develop an activity-based model and simulate a day in the life of Indians. In particular, the authors will utilise the 2019 Indian Time Use Survey to model an individual’s daily activity plan as a Markov Process and consequently simulate transitions between daily activities such as work, education, leisure, domestic chores, etc., across different times of the day, while also accounting for individual, communal, and regional factors. In doing so, this study will inform stakeholders for better urban and transportation planning tailored to unique demographic and economic landscapes of different Indian cities.

  5. MTech
    modular Adaptive Large Neighborhood Search (mALNS) for Capacitated Vehicle Routing Problem (CVRP)
    Harshvardhan Ket , and Anmol Pahwa
    Jun 2025
    Abs

    As urban environments grow increasingly complex, the Vehicle Routing Problem (VRP) continues to challenge researchers and practitioners. A typical VRP aims to configure routes for a fleet of vehicles serving a set of customers, rendering minimum operational costs. To address this computational challenge, Adaptive Large Neighborhood Search (ALNS) has emerged as an effective tool, leveraging its flexible paradigm of iterative destroy and rebuild. Specifically, the ALNS metaheuristic searches through the neighborhood by iteratively destroying and consequently rebuilding the solution thereby reconfiguring large portions of the solution using specific operators that are chosen adaptively in each iteration of the algorithm based on the performance of operators in the previous iterations, hence the name adaptive large neighborhood search. However, this flexibility introduces complexities in ALNS development and deployment stemming from operator selection and heuristic organization. To this end, this work introduces a modular ALNS (mALNS) framework to efficiently solve the different variants of the VRP. Unlike traditional ALNS implementations, mALNS emphasizes modular architecture, allowing seamless integration of destroy and repair operators, acceptance criteria, and adaptive mechanisms. Specifically, the ALNS implementation in this work breaks down operator and heuristic mechanisms into individual modular pieces, that are combined adaptively in each iteration of the algorithm based on the performance of the modules in the previous iterations. This modularity enhances scalability and adaptability, enabling tailored solutions for complex real-world routing problems that include resource-related constraints, customer-specific time-windows, multi-echelon distribution structures, as well as dynamic and stochastic elements from the delivery environment.

    Construction Consolidation Centres for Urban Construction Logistics
    Krishnapriya Mohanan , and Anmol Pahwa
    Jan 2025
    Abs

    The Indian construction industry faces significant material supply chain challenges due to its highly fragmented and decentralized delivery model, resulting in a high number of daily deliveries, coordination inefficiencies, traffic congestion, limited onsite storage, etc., while construction consolidation centres have demonstrated remarkable success in streamlining logistics and improving supply chain performances in developed countries, their applicability and potential adoption remain underexplored within India’s rapidly growing construction industry.   8. PhD
    Assessing sustainability of last-mile distribution strategies for e-commerce deliveries across different Indian cities
    Blessy K , and Anmol Pahwa
    Jul 2025
    Abs

    The surge in internet access in the past decade has paved the way for retailers to expand their market horizon with e-commerce platforms. This rise of e-commerce has bridged the gap between the consumer and the retailer, thus improving economic viability in urban freight – first pillar of sustainability. Further, e-commerce has enabled demand consolidation and delivery route optimization, thereby enhancing environmental efficiency in urban freight – second pillar of sustainability. Moreover, e-commerce has facilitated access to vital products for otherwise disadvantaged communities, thus advancing social equity in urban freight – third pillar of sustainability. This sustainable growth potential of e-commerce has drawn a growing number of retailers to compete for market through increasingly consumer-focused services. Often such consumer-focused services necessitate frequent less-than-truckload last-mile deliveries. As a consequence, urban freight witnesses a substantial increase in distribution cost as well as transportation-related negative externalities including greenhouse gas emissions advancing climate change, and criteria pollutant emissions affecting health of communities located close to logistics clusters. Hence, consumer-focused services significantly worsen the economic viability, environmental efficiency, and social equity in urban freight. Thus, to leverage the opportunities and cope with the challenges associated with last-mile deliveries, e-retailers establish alternate last-mile distribution strategies. Unlike the conventional strategy that includes use of diesel vehicles for home deliveries, these alternate strategies could include use of electric delivery vehicles for last-mile operations, a fleet of crowdsourced delivery vehicles for last-mile delivery, consolidation facilities coupled with light-duty delivery vehicles for a multi-echelon distribution, collection-points for customer pickup, or even drones and robots for autonomous operations.     The conventional approach to modelling last-mile distribution operations involves use of the discrete formulation method which renders a representative mathematical model necessitating use of sophisticated solution techniques. While such an intensive approach is justified when decision-makers require a precise plan to support operational planning, this level of precision is redundant for strategic planning, wherein available information is representative but not necessarily exact. To this end, continuous approximation (CA) method offers a practical alternative with use of continuous density functions that estimate parameters approximately, thus striking a balance between estimation accuracy and computational effort. However, typical CA-based routing frameworks assume a simplified distribution environment that significantly limits the capability of such frameworks to accurately model last-mile operations in real-world distribution environments, such as postal service, e-commerce, q-commerce, waste collection, emergency service etc. Thus, the objective of this work is to develop a holistic CA framework for the Vehicle Routing Problem (VRP) to facilitate strategic last-mile distribution planning within diverse logistic settings. Specifically, this study synthesises a range of distribution environments, each with peculiar distribution structure and distinctive customer characteristics. Subsequently, it optimizes last-mile distribution for distribution environment instance using an Adaptive Large Neighbourhood Search (ALNS) metaheuristic. Finally, this work develops a robust CA-based functional form through Symbolic Regression to precisely estimate the total distribution tour length across the various distribution environments. In doing so, this work supports strategic decision-making for a wide scope of logistic operators that may have representative but not necessarily exact information.
